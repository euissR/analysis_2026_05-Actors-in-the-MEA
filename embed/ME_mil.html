Arms transfers

The US has been the main arms provider to the region, particularly to Saudi Arabia and Israel, followed by the EU, especially to Qatar.

Arms transfers from both the US and the EU peaked in 2022 and declined in 2024. While EU transfers fell sharply in 2025, US transfers increased again.

In 2019, Russia supplied arms to Iraq and Syria, but by 2024 its transfers were limited to air systems for Iran.

Israel supplied missiles and munitions to the UAE in 2022, the only country in the region to which it transferred arms.

Between 2016–2021 and 2022–2025, four countries increased their arms transfers to the Gulf: France (+76%), Italy (+246%), South Korea (+1,564%) and the US (+115%).

Over the same period, a different group of four countries increased their arms transfers to the Middle East: France (+932%), Iran (+303%, mainly to Hezbollah and the Houthis), Türkiye (+363%) and the US (+31%).

Military bases

However, military cooperation also goes beyond arms transfers notably with the presence of military bases and military deployments.

The US has the largest military presence in the Middle East, with several bases across the region.

Deployments

Other countries also have a military presence in the region but mostly through deployments, either bilaterally or to multilateral missions such as UN peacekeeping operations, NATO missions, and the coalition against Daesh.

In 2025, the US had the largest bilateral military deployment in the region, with 20,000 personnel.

EU Member States were the largest contributors to UN peacekeeping missions in the region, with 3,247 personnel deployed in large part to UNIFIL. Italy and France provided the largest EU contingents. EU Member States also contributed 68 personnel to UNTSO and eight to UNDOF.

UNIFIL included 897 Indian, 484 Chinese, 256 South Korean and 91 Turkish personnel.

EU Member States also provided 567 personnel to the NATO Mission Iraq, compared with 34 from Türkiye and 15 from the US.

However, Türkiye had 4,000 personnel deployed bilaterally to Iraq.

In Iraq, the US contributed 2,000 personnel to the Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve and another 2,000 in Jordan.

While the EU Member States contributed around 870 personnel to the operation in Iraq, of which 300 from Italy, and 300 French and 150 German personnel in Jordan and 400 Italian personnel in Kuwait.

Military exercises

Military engagement of several countries in the region has also become particularly visible through joint military exercises, mostly bilateral.

Naval exercises occupy a central place in the region’s security architecture together with air defence.

The US has the largest footprint among external actors.

France and India have established a distinctive network of defence cooperation, particularly with the UAE and Oman.

Saudi Arabia and other GCC countries also play prominent organising roles, demonstrating growing regional ownership of Gulf security cooperation.
