World Bank-financed infrastructure-related procurement

Across World Bank-financed infrastructure procurement in Africa*, China dominated among foreign suppliers. Chinese-registered firms captured 60.8% of foreign award value, compared with 12.3% for EU27 firms.

The contrast was stronger in construction works: China accounted for nearly three quarters of foreign award value, against less than a tenth for the EU27.**

These continental figures mask a clear geographic divide. European suppliers perform much better in North Africa, while China’s advantage is concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, particularly in construction works.

Private participation in infrastructure

The picture is very different for private participation in infrastructure (PPI). Across active PPI arrangements** in Africa, EU sponsors accounted for 34.8% of the foreign sponsor-stake measure, well ahead of other African and MENA sponsors at 16.7%.

The UAE alone accounted for 7.3% (about three-quarters of the Gulf total), the United States for 6.7% and China for just 3.6%.*

Europe therefore held the largest attributed foreign sponsor share in this PPI sample, while China’s position was much stronger in the separate construction-awards sample. This points to different roles within the recorded infrastructure activity***.

The geography of infrastructure engagement also differs by instrument and actor. PPI sponsor participation is relatively dispersed across the continent,

while construction awards are more concentrated, particularly in East Africa and the Horn, which accounts for one third of foreign works-award value.

Actor patterns reinforce the contrast. EU’s PPI participation is spread across 38 countries, led by South Africa, Egypt and Morocco,

but its construction awards are much more concentrated in North Africa, with Egypt alone accounting for 38.7% of its works value.

China exhibits markedly different pattern: its PPI footprint is small and present in only 12 countries,

while its construction activity spans 35 and is especially strong in East Africa, with Tanzania, Ethiopia and Kenya together accounting for 39.2% of its works value.

Its position is also dominant within several individual borrower countries: Chinese-registered firms accounted for more than 90% of foreign works-award value in Angola, Kenya, Madagascar, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Tanzania and Ethiopia.

Some smaller external actors are even more geographically concentrated: UAE works awards are almost entirely in Nigeria and Niger,

while US works awards in this sample are confined to Somalia and Ethiopia.
