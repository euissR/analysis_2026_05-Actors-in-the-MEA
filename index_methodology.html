UNGA resolutions: UN General Assembly voting dataset (1946 to 2025) (https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4060887?ln=en), vote records for Resolution A/RES/ES-11/10 “Support for lasting peace in Ukraine” (2026) is extracted from the single record (https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4103179?ln=en)

The datasets only include resolutions for which a vote was recorded. The selected-country analysis covers 101 countries: 54 African countries, six Middle Eastern countries, six Gulf countries, China, India, Iran, Japan, Russia, South Korea, Türkiye, the US, and the current 27 EU member states.

Alignment of countries voting patterns is calculated on Russia’s voting for UNGA resolutions on Ukraine and on Israel’s vote for UNGA resolutions on occupied territories and Gaza. Alignment is calculated across all records, including those in which a country did not vote, while abstention remains included, but does not count as agreement with a no vote. Alignment with the EU is calculated as the arithmetic mean of a country’s agreement with EU member states across individual resolutions. For each resolution, agreement is the proportion of participating EU members that cast the same vote – yes, no or abstention – as the country in question. EU members that do not vote are excluded. If the country in question is an EU member, its own vote is also excluded. Resolutions on which the country does not vote are excluded. Each eligible resolution is weighted equally.

Foreign direct investment

Middle East and the Gulf

  • Data: IMF Direct Investment Positions by Counterpart Economy (release 12.0.1), inward direct investment, net (liabilities less assets), all instruments, all entities. Year-end stocks in current USD mn, 2019–24, by immediate counterpart.
  • Source rule: the host’s official value where it exists, otherwise the counterpart-derived (mirror) value, per host, counterpart and year. IMF label variants are merged before this choice (Korea, Türkiye, Netherlands and six EU "Republic of" forms).
  • Averages: each position is averaged over the years it is observed; missing years are never set to zero or carried forward. Recorded zeros stay zero and display as 0.0; negative positions are kept.
  • EU and Gulf treated alike: coverage, matching and the missing-data audit work member by member for both EU27 and the GCC.
  • Status of each value: Complete / Gaps (positions not observed in every year, or only in the other period) / Discontinuity (official and counterpart-derived values mixed, notably Saudi Arabia in 2024). Materiality test: an issue counts if it affects at least 1% of the investor’s value and USD 10 mn.
  • Saudi Arabia 2024: the data do not show how much of the fall in recorded positions reflects the change of source rather than investment movements, so affected 2022–24 values are not presented as comparable.
  • World totals: official totals are reported separately and are not lowered by missing bilateral positions; counterpart-derived totals (Iraq, Jordan, Oman, Qatar, Syria, UAE, Yemen; Saudi Arabia in 2024) equal the sum of counterpart positions, so missing positions lower them one for one.
  • Main visual: same periods for every group (2019–21 vs 2022–24); values in USD bn and shares, with rounded labels (shares to one decimal; stocks in whole USD bn from 10 bn). Markers: * high income and Middle East and the Gulf, 2022–24 (Saudi Arabia’s 2024 break); † middle / low-income shares, total and other (UK–Jordan). Chart note under the visual; "Other" = other counterparts plus positions not allocated to a counterpart.
  • Sensitivity checks (supplementary only): six-year same-source matched pairs (member level; Saudi Arabia drops out; denominator = recipients with a constant World source), 2022–23 window, constant coverage 2019–23, excluding Saudi Arabia, excluding the UK position in Jordan.
  • Robustness of the trend readings: 8 of 10 high- and middle/low-income share readings keep their direction in every test; the US and Asian-four shares in high income are mixed, as are EU stocks.
  • Warnings carried: UK–Jordan (about USD 8 bn recorded only in 2021–23, moving the low/middle-income total in both periods); UK-reported positions missing in 2024 in Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Syria and the UAE; Hungary and Luxembourg positions in the UAE fall by USD 77 bn in 2024 (recorded, kept).

Africa

  • Same rules as the Middle East: data, official-first source rule per host, counterpart and year, observed-year averages, statuses with the 1% / USD 10 mn materiality test, EU27 and GCC built member by member, regional aggregate counterparts excluded, same label rounding.
  • Eight switching economies: Benin, Cabo Verde, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal and Togo switch from official to counterpart-derived data in 2023; Eswatini, Guinea-Bissau and Tanzania in 2024. Their recorded total falls from USD 53.7 bn (2022) to USD 26.0 bn (2024), and Gulf positions there disappear after the switch. All 2022–24 values carry an asterisk, explained in a footnote; every direction of change is also tested without these economies.
  • Host-share measures (leading investor, single-investor majorities, GDP-weighted shares): an investor counts only if observed in each year of the period (for EU27 and the GCC, at least one member each year); Sudan and South Sudan are excluded. The GDP-weighted shares use 51 economies: Somalia and Guinea-Bissau do have GDP data, so they are added, while Eritrea and South Sudan have none. The counts in the text are unchanged: 18 of 52 economies (EU 12, China 5, Türkiye 1 = Somalia, on a total averaging USD 11 mn); the EU leads 35 economies (86% of measured GDP) and China 13 (5.5% of stock, 12% of GDP).
  • World totals and coverage: 37 of 54 economies have a counterpart-derived total in at least one year of 2022–24. That includes 15 of the 18 single-investor majorities, and Egypt in every year. Only Kuwait among the GCC states reports outward positions, so Gulf values are lower bounds. Recorded positions add up to slightly more than the World totals (the "not allocated" part of "Other" is −3% in 2022–24), because counterpart-derived totals fall whenever a position is missing.
  • Robustness: 7 tests: recorded, excluding 2024, constant coverage 2019–23, matched pairs, excluding the switching economies, excluding Mauritius, and excluding the Netherlands position in South Africa. The fall in the EU share and the rises in the Gulf and Indian shares hold in every test; India’s rise is only +0.2 pp without Mauritius. The US, Chinese and Japanese shares are mixed or flat, so they are described as levels.
  • Warnings carried:
    • The Netherlands position in South Africa: USD 25.8 bn (2020), 58.5 bn (2021), 19.7 bn (2023).
    • The Netherlands position in Angola: USD 10.0 bn (2022), 2.5 bn (2023).
    • A UK position in Egypt of USD 5.0 bn, recorded only in 2024.
    • Mauritius holds 36% of recorded stock.

Official development assistance and other official finance

  • Data: OECD DAC2A (ODA disbursements), DAC2B (other official flows, OOF) and DAC3A (ODA commitments), 2019–24. All OECD values are in USD mn at constant 2024 prices (PRICE_BASE=Q). Chinese finance is analysed separately using AidData CLG-LMIC 1.0.
  • Measures: Net ODA is DAC2A measure 206 (net disbursements), not the OECD’s headline grant-equivalent measure of donor effort. ODA commitments use DAC3A measure 305 and are kept separate from disbursements. Gross official finance is constructed as ODA grants (201) + gross ODA loans (204) + OOF grants (2201) + gross OOF loan disbursements (2204). OOF equity (2217) is excluded.
  • Periods: OECD comparisons use cumulative three-year totals for 2019–21 and 2022–24, not annual averages. Both windows contain three years, so annualising them would not change percentage differences. AidData uses a separate five-year window (2019–23).
  • Africa-wide benchmark: The OECD Africa recipient aggregate (F) minus country-coded Saint Helena (SHN), applied consistently to every donor and aggregate series. Regional and unspecified allocations are retained. Africa-wide totals are not reconstructed from country-level observations and are not reduced because recipient allocations are unavailable.
  • Geography: Country and subregional comparisons use 53 country-coded African recipients, assigned to five project subregions. Regional and unspecified allocations are excluded from this layer, so the five subregions do not necessarily sum to the Africa-wide benchmark. Donor-level geographical shares use the relevant country’s allocated portfolio as their denominator.
  • Names and donor mapping: All joins use OECD donor and recipient codes, not text labels. TUR = Türkiye, KOR = South Korea, ARE = UAE; recipient ISO3 codes distinguish similarly named countries, notably the two Congos. Europe comprises the 27 EU member states plus EU Institutions (4EU001, including the Commission and EIB); the United Kingdom (GBR) is outside EU27.
  • Aggregation and double counting: Donors are assigned to mutually exclusive categories. The ten selected actor/bloc entries are compared separately from the unranked “Other bilateral” category (13 donors). The latter is already included in sovereign-donor totals and is never added again. Other multilateral institutions exclude EU Institutions. Private donors (9PRIV0) and overlapping aggregate donor categories are not added to official-provider totals. ODA commitments and OOF memo totals are likewise never added to gross disbursements.
  • Missing data and coverage: Only reported observations enter the recorded totals; unreported finance is not estimated or carried forward. Saudi Arabia has Africa-wide and Middle East-wide data but no recipient-level allocation, so its country and subregional cells remain blank, not zero. There are no Saudi Arabian OOF observations in the OECD’s DAC2B file; its reported grant/loan composition therefore reflects ODA flows only. Other absent reporting, including for India and Russia, must not be interpreted as zero engagement. Recorded negative net flows are retained (notably Kuwait in 2022–24).
  • Country-allocation gaps: Regional or unspecified allocations remain substantial for some providers. In 2022–24, 21.0% of Europe’s recorded net ODA, 10.2% of US net ODA and 26.2% of “Other bilateral” net ODA were not allocated to individual recipients in the country-coded layer. Saudi Arabia’s country allocation is entirely unavailable. Geographic concentration and country leadership therefore describe recorded country-coded finance, not necessarily donors’ complete portfolios.
  • China: AidData CLG-LMIC 1.0, using recommended adjusted commitments in constant 2023 USD for 2019–23. The USD 47.2bn Africa figure includes country-allocated projects only; USD 2.71bn in separately coded Africa-regional commitments is excluded. ODA-like, OOF-like and unclassified commitments remain distinct. AidData’s adjustment avoids aggregation of overlapping financing or rollover arrangements. These commitments are neither added to OECD totals nor ranked directly against OECD disbursements.
  • Checks and sensitivity: The three raw OECD extracts were checked for duplicate donor–recipient–year–measure keys; none were found in the constant-price series. All 224 source-level reconciliation checks passed, alongside the actor-group and “Other bilateral” identities. The principal gross-finance comparison excludes OOF equity: including it would reduce the reported all-provider increase from 2.6% to approximately 2.2%, without changing its direction.
  • Interpretation: Rankings apply only to selected reporting actors, not all external providers. Grant/loan shares describe recorded gross flows, not the concessionality of individual loans or outstanding debt exposure. Net ODA, gross official finance and commitments measure different things. Recipient attribution does not necessarily establish where expenditure physically occurred. The constructed gross-finance measure includes OOF irrespective of developmental purpose and should not be presented as identical to the OECD’s narrower official development finance (ODF) indicator.
