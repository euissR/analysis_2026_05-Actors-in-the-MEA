Diplomatic visits

Between 2019 and July 2026, the US (110), Türkiye (101), Iran (81) and the EU (72) accounted for most high-level visits to the Middle East and the Gulf.

 

US visits focused on Israel and Saudi Arabia;

 

Türkiye’s on Qatar and Saudi Arabia;

 

Iran’s on Qatar and Iraq;

 

and the EU’s on Jordan and the UAE. Visits among GCC countries intensified in 2024–2025, while Iran increased its visits to the region in 2025.

 

Partnership hierarchy

The Middle East and the Gulf are priority partners for China, Türkiye and the US. China has comprehensive strategic partnerships with Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, and an innovative comprehensive partnership with Israel. Most countries in the region are major non-NATO allies of the US, while Israel is also a strategic partner.

Türkiye has high-level partnerships with Qatar, Iraq, Jordan and the UAE, with its Supreme Strategic Committee with Qatar representing the highest level.

 

India has a comprehensive strategic partnership with the UAE and strategic partnerships with Israel, Kuwait, Oman and Saudi Arabia.

 

Russia has a strategic partnership with the UAE and strategic cooperation with Saudi Arabia.

 

The EU has Strategic and Comprehensive Partnerships with Egypt and Jordan and a strategic partnership with the GCC. It is also negotiating individual strategic partnerships with GCC countries: negotiations with Qatar and the UAE were launched in December 2025, followed by Bahrain in July 2026.

 

The EU and the US have held summits with the GCC since 2024 and 2015, respectively.

 

China launched its summit with the League of Arab States in 2022, with the next scheduled for 2026. Russia sought to organise a summit with the Arab states, but it was postponed indefinitely.

 

Between 2022 and 2026, Israel was the only country in the region that mostly voted against Russia’s position. Until 2024, Syria consistently voted with Russia; following the fall of the Assad regime, it either abstained or was absent.

 

Countries in the Middle East and the Gulf consistently voted against Israel’s position on all relevant UNGA resolutions from 2023 to 2025, as they had on resolutions concerning the territories occupied by Israel, including the Golan Heights and East Jerusalem, between 2016 and 2026.
