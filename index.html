Talking power: diplomatic ties and political influence

In Algeria, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Senegal and Tunisia, almost every external actor considered has an embassy. Physical presence, however, does not automatically translate into influence.

This section analyses diplomatic ties through five lenses: embassies, high-level visits between 2019 and 2026, summits, formal partnerships, and voting patterns at the UN General Assembly on selected issues.

Embassies

China has embassies in all 53 countries with which it has diplomatic relations.

EU Delegations are present in 50 countries.

The US has embassies in 49.

Algeria, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Senegal and Tunisia stand out as crowded diplomatic arenas, with 15 of these external actors maintaining an embassy in each country.

High-level visits

In addition to permanent diplomatic representation, high-level visits also underline the concentration of diplomatic capital.

High-level visits are strongly concentrated in North Africa, which accounted for 45.7% of all visits.

The EU visited Africa more often from 2019 to 2026, with 75 visits across 25 African countries. Its leading destinations included Egypt (15 visits), Ethiopia (7), and Kenya, Rwanda and South Africa (5 each).

Türkiye recorded 54 visits across 24 countries, but half were to North Africa – particularly Egypt (13), Algeria (7) and Libya (5) – while another 15 were to West Africa.

China made 43 visits across 29 countries, giving it the widest and most geographically balanced coverage: 11 visits were to Southern Africa and the Indian Ocean, 10 each to West and East Africa, seven to North Africa and five to Central Africa.

India showed a stronger focus on Southern Africa, directing 13 of its 27 visits to Southern Africa and the Indian Ocean, especially South Africa and Mauritius.

So did Japan, with 12 of 30 visits.

And South Korea, with four of 10.

Summits

Diplomatic outreach has also multiplied through summits with the African continent.

The AU–EU summits are the only summits in a continent-to-continent format.

But summit diplomacy with Africa is becoming increasingly crowded, especially in the Africa+1 format.

Established frameworks with the EU, China and Japan have proved relatively reliable and recurrent, while newer initiatives by Russia, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia and South Korea have been more intermittent.

Iran has focused mainly on economic diplomacy since 2023, while Israel’s proposed Africa summit in 2017 was postponed and never held.

Partnerships

Embassies, visits and summits, however, do not automatically translate into partnerships of equal depth or type. Several external actors have deeper ties with some countries.

China stands out for the breadth of its formalised partnerships, although these vary in depth. Other actors formalise partnerships more selectively.

The EU has 11 partnerships with African countries, of which three strategic partnerships and several on CRMs or value chains.

The US identifies four African countries as major non-NATO allies (Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt and Kenya) out of seven identified partnerships in Africa.

The UAE focuses its partnerships with African countries on the economy through seven comprehensive economic partnership agreements.

India labels four partnerships as strategic and one with Mauritius as enhanced strategic partnership. Russia and Türkiye four each.

Russia labels its partnerships with Algeria, Egypt and South Africa as strategic.

The DRC, Egypt, Morocco, Nigeria and South Africa have the highest concentration of partnerships with the external actors considered.

Voting at the UN General Assembly

The extent and depth of partnerships does not automatically translate into alignment. On the contrary, with their 54 votes at the UN General Assembly, African states express significant agency in their voting patterns. Abstention is by far the predominant position among African states, reflecting a general tendency towards non-alignment.

However, on average African countries abstained slightly less in the period 2022–2026 (46%) than in 2016–2021 (51%).

However, on average African states align more frequently with the EU than with Russia on Ukraine.

African countries aligning with the EU more than 50% of times include Cabo Verde, Liberia, and Seychelles.

Those aligning more frequently with Russia include Eritrea, Sudan, Burundi, Zimbabwe and Mali.

However, while Burundi’s alignment with Russia decreased after 2022, Mali’s alignment with Russia increased.

Africa aligns more often with the EU also on other issues such as the territories occupied by Israel and Palestinian territories*.

Between 2016 and 2025, African countries abstained on average in 13% of UN General Assembly votes on resolutions concerning the Israeli-occupied territories and did not vote in 23%.

On average, African countries aligned more often with the EU, with the alignment rate increasing from 54% between 2016 and 2022 to 59% between 2023 and 2025.

Since 2023, African countries have abstained in 6% of UN General Assembly votes on Gaza and the Palestinian territories* and did not vote in 18%.

Alignment with Israel has been close to zero, while alignment with the EU has averaged 78%.
