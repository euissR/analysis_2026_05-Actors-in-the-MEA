# Handoff brief: Flourish → vanilla JS / D3 scrollytelling

## Context

I'm cd_trich at EUISS (Brussels). I'm building a multi-chapter scrollytelling piece on foreign actors in Africa and the Middle East/Gulf. The chapters are diplomatic, economic, soft power and military. Africa and MENA/Gulf are two separate pieces with identical chart types and different data. Output is static files (GitHub Pages, embedded in Drupal).

I prototyped everything in Flourish and hit its limits:

- No object permanence between story slides, so no smooth transitions.
- Map and header positions jump between slides.
- No per-row opacity, no legend relabelling, no real "all" filter.
- Facets share one y-domain.
- Many workarounds (RGBA colour keys, duplicated vis per step).

So we're rebuilding in D3/vanilla JS. R (euissR/euissGeo) stays upstream for wrangling and exports tidy files.

## Workflow I want

I give you the chapter text (so you see the scrolly structure) and the data. You build the graphics and the scroll steps.

## Agreed architecture

- One function per chart type, same contract: `chart = makeX(el, data, opts)`, then `chart.update(state, {duration})`.
- Scroll steps are data, not code: `{id, chart, state: {highlight: [...]}, text}`.
- D3 joins on stable keys (ISO3, actor) so elements persist and transition between steps.
- Modules:
  - `geoMap`: fixed-viewport projected basemap with pluggable layers (proportional dots, spikes, categorical fills, labels).
  - `smallMultiples`: facet wrapper with shared scales.
  - Marks: stacked column, arrow/dumbbell, area/line, tile/heatmap, strip plot.
  - Shared: `theme.js`, highlight helper, tooltip, legend, scroll engine.
- Scroll engine: sticky stage with a fixed aspect ratio, so nothing jumps between steps. Use IntersectionObserver or scrollama.
- Region config: one codebase, per-region data and viewport.
  - MENA/Gulf reference centre: lon 44, lat 24, tightly zoomed on the Gulf.
  - Africa reference centre: lon 20, lat 0.
- Build order: skeleton + theme + scroll engine, then geoMap with diplomatic presence, then small-multiple stacked columns (UN votes), then arrows, then the rest section by section.

## Design system

- Font: PT Sans Narrow.
- Palette (EUISS):
  - teal #309ebe, fuchsia #df3144, navy #113655, orange #f28d22
  - base grey #595959, light grey/grid #c6c6c6, near-white #f9f9f9
  - ink #1d1d1b, muted text #646363
- Maps: white land on light-grey ocean, thin light-grey borders, no graticule or datum.
- Axes: no axis titles, no axis lines, gridlines kept. Small multiples show only first and last year on x. Y tick labels are hidden when the 0–100 range is implicit.
- Tooltip: bold name on line 1, metric on line 2 (e.g. "**Kenya**<br>12% align with Israel").
- Highlight/dim: the highlighted item keeps its colour, everything else drops to 20% opacity of its own colour. Never swap the colour to grey.
- Legend: clean labels only, no title, no entries for dimmed states.
- Titles are short with no year ranges. Subtitle states the metric and period.

## Chart inventory (per region)

**Diplomatic**

- Embassy presence: dots, switchable by actor.
- High-level visits: dots sized by count, switchable by actor.
- Partnership hierarchy: ranking 1–3 per actor-country.
- Summit timeline: start/last/next-summit dots on a line, size = number of summits at start, highlighted by actor.
- UN votes: country-level small multiples, stacked columns (align / abstain / absent). Align = teal, abstain #595959, absent #c6c6c6. Fixed 0–100 y-axis. The gap above the stack IS non-alignment, so never normalise to 100%.
- Actor-level UN view: TBD.

**Economic**

- Trade shares and balances: period averages 2019–21 vs 2022–24, shown as arrows or dumbbells. Period colours: 2019–21 #595959, 2022–24 #df3144.
- Import dependency (tech, food, energy): arrows per partner.
- FDI shares: arrows, faceted by income group, with highlight steps (EU, US, Gulf, China/Japan/India/South Korea).
- ODA/OOF: stacked bars by actor and window.
- Debt: stacked areas by creditor.

**Soft power:** six maps (languages/cultural organisations, sectors).

**Military**

- Arms transfers: stacked by supplier over time.
- Bases: dot map.
- Deployments: spike maps for bilateral, multilateral (EU highlight) and PMC.
- Exercises: strip plot by lead country and type, bilateral vs multilateral as shape.

**Overview:** countries vs actors map. Categories are Country, Actor and Both (a blended tone for overlap), at 50% opacity.

## Data contract (R → JS)

- Tidy long CSV/JSON per chart, named like `dipl_5_un_isr_AF.csv`.
- ISO3_CODE is the join key. Percentages are 0–100.
- Missing values are truly blank, never the string "NA". Call `st_drop_geometry()` before exporting any sf table.
- Sort in R as needed (e.g. alphabetical by name). No dummy placeholder rows, and stray out-of-region rows (e.g. IRN in Africa files) are dropped at source.
- Collapse multi-row cases in R (e.g. Palestine with several presence types, or several partnership tiers per country: max per actor-country).
- Basemap: unprojected WGS84 TopoJSON. D3 projects (Robinson via d3-geo-projection) and fits to a fixed per-region extent.

## Geo gotchas learned the hard way

- GeoJSON must be lon/lat (RFC 7946). Never export a projected CRS.
- Run `st_wrap_dateline()` on unprojected data only. Russia is the classic breaker.
- Always chain `st_make_valid()` after `st_simplify()`. Without it, s2 throws "Loop X edge Y crosses loop Z edge W".
- Wrong ring winding makes oceans fill in (same symptom as the Russia bug). TopoJSON avoids this.
- Disputed-territory codes in the country list (XF, XG, XU, XV, XXS) won't match polygons. Keep or drop deliberately.

## Naming

R objects use a topic prefix (`mena_eco_tra_*`). Keep chart IDs parallel to data names (`dipl_5_un_isr`).
